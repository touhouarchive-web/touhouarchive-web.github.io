Introduction to Touhou

Touhou Project is a long-running Japanese game series created by ZUN under Team Shanghai Alice. Its world mixes folkloric beings, spell-card duels, unusual incidents, and a soundtrack with a life of its own.

What makes Touhou distinctive?

The core games are mostly vertical-scrolling danmaku shoot-’em-ups. Players dodge patterned barrages, battle stage bosses, and uncover the incident behind each game. Official books and manga develop the same world through other formats.

弾幕 · Gameplay

Danmaku

The screen becomes a pattern to read: gaps, timing, movement, and route choice matter as much as raw firepower.

幻想郷 · Setting

Gensokyo

A hidden land inhabited by humans, youkai, spirits, gods, and other beings. Its relationship with the Outside World is shaped by the Great Hakurei Barrier.

異変 · Stories

Incidents

Many games begin when something abnormal threatens or unsettles Gensokyo. Reimu and Marisa are frequent investigators, but other protagonists and perspectives appear across releases.

音楽 · Music

Music

ZUN’s compositions are a defining part of the series. A single original track may inspire many fan arrangements, which should be credited as adaptations rather than originals.

Official print works

Official books and manga expand the setting with records, interviews, serialized narratives, and character perspectives. They are essential for deeper lore research.

二次創作 · Fan culture

Fan-created culture

Touhou permits and encourages a large fan-work ecosystem under published guidelines. Fan games, arrangements, art, and memes are culturally important, but they are not automatically canon.

How to read these entries

Each entry is an introductory stub for the wider encyclopedia. The production version of a detailed lore article should add its official first appearance, original Japanese wording, citations to the exact scene or page, and any uncertainty around translations or chronology.

A beginner’s path

Use these checks when expanding an entry:

  • Learn the basic world and main protagonists
  • Try a game or watch an authorized playthrough
  • Read the story dialogue and endings
  • Use official print works for added detail
  • Cross-check claims before treating them as canon
  • Enjoy fan works—while labelling them as fan works
